"A life in recollection is the most perfect imaginable; memory gives you your fill more abundantly than all of reality and has a security which no reality possesses. A life-situation recalled has already passed into eternity and has no more temporal interest."

"In the baptism of forgetfulness I plunge everything experienced into the eternity of remembrance; everything finite and contingent is forgotten and erased. Then I sit thoughtful like an old man, grey-headed, and in a low voice, almost a whisper, explain the pictures; and by my side a child sits and listens, even though he remembers everything before I tell it."

*— Søren Kierkegaard, Either/Or*

---

**Connection to [[I will meet you yet again -Amrita Pritam]]:**

Pritam and Kierkegaard meet at the same impossible crossroads — the place where the body ends but memory refuses to. Pritam's "threads of memory woven with enduring specks" and Kierkegaard's "eternity of remembrance" both insist that what was truly lived is not subject to death; it has already passed into a more secure form than reality itself ever possessed. Where Pritam promises a future reunion through gathered particles, Kierkegaard describes the same operation in reverse: the past recalled becomes eternal, no longer temporal, no longer perishable. Both transform loss into the raw material of an encounter that cannot be prevented.
