> Knowledge can in part be set aside, and one can then go further in order to collect new; the natural scientist can set aside insects and flowers and then go further, but if the existing person sets aside the decision in existence, it is *eo ipso* lost, and he is changed.

— Søren Kierkegaard, *Papers*, VI B 66 (1845)

---

**Connection to [[The function of prayer]]:**

Where the prayer note says that praying changes the one who prays rather than God, this passage supplies the mechanism. Prayer is an act of *existence*, not of knowledge — you cannot bracket it, research it, and return to the same self afterward. The decision in existence is not kept in storage like a specimen; the moment it is deferred it is forfeited, and the person who deferred it is not the person who could have made it. Loss and transformation are the same event — one proposition about knowledge, one about the impossibility of suspending a decision.
