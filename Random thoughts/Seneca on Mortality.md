> You will die, not because you are ill, but because you are alive; even when you have been cured, the same end awaits you; when you have recovered, it will be not death, but ill-health, that you have escaped.

— Seneca, *Moral Letters to Lucilius* 78 ("On the healing power of the mind")

---

**Connection to [[Svaki dan - jedan život]]:**

Both passages answer the same anxiety — the fear of what is still to come — by moving the whole weight of living into the present. The Serbian note draws the conclusion in the imperative: *"пожури да живиш, и мисли да је сваки поједини дан уједно и твој поједини живот"* — hurry to live, and count each single day as a whole life. Seneca's line supplies the ground for that urgency from the opposite direction: he removes health itself as a hedge against death. Recovery is not a reprieve, only an escape from illness, not from the end. Once the illness is set aside as the thing you are afraid of, what remains is the bare fact of being alive — which is the only reason you will die, and therefore the only thing you have to spend. The delay the note warns against ("увек постоји нешто што одлажемо за сутрашњи дан") has nothing left to hide behind.
