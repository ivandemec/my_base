> *"As the activity of the mystic must end in a via negativa, a theology of God's absence, a craving for the cloud of unknowing beyond knowledge and for the silence beyond speech, so art must tend toward anti-art, the elimination of the 'subject,' the substitution of chance for intention, and the pursuit of silence."*

Sontag's essay traces the modern artist's drive toward silence — not as mere absence, but as a radical act of negation that paradoxically intensifies meaning. Where Neruda imagines a collective, planetary stillness that could "interrupt this sadness" of human violence and relentless motion, Sontag sees silence as the individual artist's ultimate gesture: the renunciation (Rimbaud, Wittgenstein, Duchamp) that retroactively charges everything previously said with heightened authority. Both refuse to equate silence with death — Neruda insists "I want no truck with death," and Sontag distinguishes silence as *preparation* from silence as *termination*. What unites them is the intuition that only by stopping — by relinquishing the compulsion to speak, to move, to produce — can something genuinely new enter the world.

---

**Connection to [[KEEPING QUIET by Pablo Neruda]]:**

Both texts explore silence not as emptiness but as a creative and existential force. Neruda's poem calls for a shared, global pause — a stillness that could interrupt violence and sadness. Sontag's essay frames silence as the artist's radical gesture of negation, a renunciation that charges all prior speech with new meaning. Both distinguish silence from death and inactivity, seeing it instead as a threshold through which something genuinely new might enter the world.
