
“Who are you, then? Tell me?”

Goethe’s Faust answers with one of literature’s most intriguing paradoxes: “A part of that power which eternally wills evil, and eternally does good.” The words are spoken by Mephistopheles, whose identity is defined through contradiction—destruction and creation, darkness and unintended good.

  

The passage captures a central tension of Faust: human beings and forces around them are rarely simple. Good and evil, intention and consequence, knowledge and ignorance can exist in uneasy relationship with one another.

  

Goethe’s work invites us to look beyond appearances and question what truly defines a person—their intentions, their actions, or the consequences they leave behind.

  

📖 Reference: Johann Wolfgang von Goethe, Faust, Part I, “Study” / Mephistopheles scene (wording varies by translation).
