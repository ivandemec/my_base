“Silence is only frightening to people who are compulsively verbalizing. […] Personally I find nothing upsetting about silence at all. In fact it can’t get too quiet for me.”

--William S. Burroughs

  

This quote comes from “The Job,” although Burroughs expressed his belief that silence was important many times. He of course believed that language was a virus and that this meant people were compulsively verbalising—not only speaking aloud but with an internal voice. As words essentially controlled people, silence was resistance to that control. He believed silence was highly beneficial, perhaps even leading to the cure of certain diseases, and would try to sit silently and halt his “subvocal speech” whenever possible.
