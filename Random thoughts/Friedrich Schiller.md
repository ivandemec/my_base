> *"Man plays only when he is in the full sense of the word a man, and he is only wholly Man when he is playing."*

> *"Man is only serious with the agreeable, the good, the perfect; but with Beauty he plays. Yet we must not think of play as what goes on in play, and only the beautiful can make him complete... in every condition of humanity it is precisely play, and play alone, that makes man complete and displays at once his twofold nature."*

Schiller's argument is the philosophical twin of the note on play: he insists that play is not a childish leftover but the only state in which the sense impulse and the form impulse — instinct and reason — stop colliding and are reconciled. His "Man is only fully a human being when he plays" is the exact counter-image of the line about people growing old *because* they stop playing. And where play is called "tiha subverzija" against operationalized, functionalized life, Schiller calls Art "a daughter of Freedom" that soars above necessity — both refuse to let a human be reduced to an executor of tasks.

---

**Connection to [[Igra]]:**

Both texts place play at the center of what makes a human whole. Schiller's three impulses (sense, form, play) describe precisely the moment "nagon i razum privremeno prestaju da se sudaraju" — the play impulse as the reconciliation of instinct and reason. Where the note says people age because they stop playing, Schiller says man is only wholly Man when playing: play is not recreation but the condition of completeness itself.
