> We are not immune to the lure of wonder and mystery and awe: we have music and art and literature, and find that the serious ethical dilemmas are better handled by Shakespeare and Tolstoy and Schiller and Dostoyevsky and George Eliot than in the mythical morality tales of the holy books. Literature, not scripture, sustains the mind and — since there is no other metaphor — also the soul.

— Christopher Hitchens, *God Is Not Great: How Religion Poisons Everything*, Chapter 1: Putting It Mildly

---

**Connection to [[Lightning Struck a Flock of Witches]]:**

Beard's painting stages the moment the supernatural meets the natural world — a flock of witches, mid-flight, broken apart by a bolt of lightning. It is the sublime rendered as spectacle, awe operating through image rather than argument. Hitchens's claim sits right beside it: wonder and mystery are not the property of scripture, and the human response to them — music, art, literature — carries the moral weight that mythic tales pretend to monopolise. Both say that art is where the numinous gets handled honestly.
