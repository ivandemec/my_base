> I live from day to day, and content myself with having enough to meet my present and ordinary needs; for the extraordinary, all the provision in the world could not suffice.

---

**Connection to [[Montaigne on Solitude]]:**

The same self-sufficiency Montaigne builds in solitude is what lets him live from day to day. If the room behind the shop teaches you to do without, then provision against the extraordinary becomes pointless — you have already rehearsed the loss. The quote is the practical corollary of the essay: no hoarding, no anxious foresight, only the present need met.
