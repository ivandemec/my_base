> *"Everything is possible, and yet nothing is. All is permitted, and yet again, nothing. No matter which way we go, it is no better than any other. It is all the same whether you achieve something or not, have faith or not, just as it is all the same whether you cry or remain silent. Be a snowflake dancing in the air, a flower floating downstream! Have courage when you don't need to, and be a coward when you must be brave! Who knows? You may still be a winner! […] All gain is a loss, and all loss is a gain. Love your unhappiness and hate your happiness, mix everything up, scramble it all!"*

— Emil Cioran, *On the Heights of Despair*, chapter "Nothing Matters"

---

**Connection to [[The Passion for the Absurd]]:**

Your note dwells in the gravity of the absurd — suffering as absolute, spirit as wound, negation pushed to its final expression. This passage is the same realization inverted into lightness: once *nothing matters*, the demonic seriousness can suddenly dissolve into the grace of a snowflake "dancing in the air." Where "The Passion for the Absurd" turns the collapse of all reasons into intensity and venom, "Nothing Matters" turns it into play — two postures of the same freedom.
