> "The truth is always something that is told, not something that is known. If there were no speaking or writing, there would be no truth about anything. There would only be what is."

— Susan Sontag, *The Benefactor*, Ch. 1 (1963)

[The Benefactor — Wikiquote](https://en.wikiquote.org/wiki/Susan_Sontag)

---

**Connection to [[Beware of anything that you hear yourself saying often.]]:**

The source note warns against the phrases you catch yourself repeating — the verbal habits that harden until a saying feels like a fact. Sontag supplies the metaphysics behind that suspicion: truth is not a substance lying in wait to be known, it is *made* in the telling. A phrase repeated often enough becomes indistinguishable from truth precisely because there is nothing underneath it to contradict — no speaking, no writing, and there would be "only what is." What Sontag calls the told-ness of truth is exactly what the warning is aimed at: the slogan you have heard yourself say too many times has stopped being a claim about the world and become load-bearing, a piece of truth-manufacture running on nothing but its own momentum.
